# Fastest Dude Alive

An original, browser-first speedster game built with TypeScript and Babylon.js. Run the streets, climb the skyline, cross the river on foot, and decide who gets help when a city's emergency network fails.

**Current build:** a 5.55 km-wide Meridian, ten districts, fifteen landmarks, independent free roam and a fifteen-chapter campaign, **Minutes Owed**. The recently rebuilt crimson speedster is retained; the overhaul concentrates on the world, presentation, responsiveness and things to do.

## Play

```bash
npm ci --ignore-scripts
npm run dev
```

Requires Node.js 20.19+ or 22.12+. Open the Vite URL in a browser with WebGPU or WebGL 2. All models, textures and sounds are generated by the game; no art downloads or account are needed.

## Free roam

The whole city is open immediately, independently of campaign progress.

- **37 × 37 blocks**, up from 25 × 25: 2.19 times the previous square map area. The original inner-city tower footprints, roof heights and landmark approaches are retained.
- **Ten districts:** the Crest, Halcyon Row, Old Meridian, Kestrel Docks, Marrow Hill and Midtown, plus Northline, Westhaven, the Foundry Belt and Saltmere.
- **Fifteen landmarks**, including a northern observatory and railway station, Westhaven reservoir, a foundry exchange, the Saltmere freight terminal and Beacon Point.
- **Nine time trials:** Meridian Loop, Riverline Sprint, Crest Ladder, Kestrel Courier, Northline Express, Westhaven Circuit, Foundry Night Shift, Saltmere Coast and Five Bridges. The four original course geometries keep existing personal bests comparable.
- **Personal-best ghosts:** a cyan pace runner follows your fastest recorded run. Recordings stay in your browser, at five samples per second, up to four minutes and eight courses. Times remain saved when older ghosts are evicted. Recovery-assisted runs finish as practice and do not overwrite a best.
- **Six rescues:** Warehouse Collapse, Container Stack Failure, Last Train Out, Reservoir Evacuation, Shift Change and The Stranded Ferry.
- **112 resonance motes.** The original 64 IDs and placement distribution are retained; another 48 populate the outer boroughs.
- **Six opt-in duels**, including Vantage at Beacon Point. Combat begins only when you start an encounter.
- Moving traffic and walking citizens populate the streets around you, independently of rescue actors. They are ambient scenery rather than collision obstacles.

Press **M** to inspect the full city and activity markers. Opening the map pauses the world and releases the mouse; close it with M, Escape, the controller map/pause buttons or its close button. Approach an activity and press **T** to start; T again abandons it.

## Minutes Owed

Nolan Reyes is a municipal courier who knows which addresses take longer to reach. Meridian's new emergency-routing network promises to change that. When a field rescue rig lets him cross the city faster than its dispatch system can think, he discovers the waiting times are a policy, not an accident.

The campaign follows the evidence through emergency crews, contractors, the newsroom and the people left off the map. Vance must answer for a system he designed. Iona Vale, the former rescue commander now called Vantage, is prepared to force a shutdown that Nolan will have to keep people alive through.

| Act | Chapters |
| --- | --- |
| I | A Route Home · Manual Override · Controlled Demolition · On the Record |
| II | The Waiting List · A Controlled Failure · The Price of a Street · The Missing Column |
| III | Always a Step Ahead · Everyone Is a Route · An Honest Shutdown · Minutes Owed · Open Circuit · Waterline · Every Address |

Chapter twelve offers two emergency plans with different playable tasks. Rebuilding chapters remember the choice and take the campaign into the new districts. Chapters unlock in sequence and remain replayable. Existing chapter IDs are retained for save compatibility; completing the former finale unlocks chapter thirteen. Old saves did not record the old ending choice, so the new campaign cannot recover it retroactively.

The authored script, cast and campaign runner live in `src/game/story/`. See [story design](docs/STORY.md).

## Movement and combat

Momentum, heavy steering at speed, wall running, vertical running, water running, slide, air dash and focus remain the foundation.

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| WASD / left stick | Run and steer; analog stick deflection controls walking speed |
| Shift | Sprint toward approximately 215 m/s / 775 km/h |
| Space | Jump; in the air, phase dash; on a wall, kick away |
| Ctrl / C | Slide while moving; release before starting another slide |
| Mouse / right stick | Look |
| Left click | Speed strike |
| E | Arc bolt |
| Q | Kinetic pulse |
| F (hold) | Focus time: slows enemies, civilians and traffic while you keep moving |
| T | Start / abandon nearest activity |
| R | Recover to the nearest road; active time trial becomes practice |
| M | Pause and inspect the city map |
| Escape | Pause / resume |

Jump buffering and a short coyote window make landing transitions more forgiving. Opposite steering now brakes into a turn instead of getting stuck on an exactly reversed heading. Holding slide no longer repeatedly grants its entry boost. Gamepad camera rotation uses elapsed time rather than frame count.

Attacks commit to the position shown by their warning, allowing a dodge. Charges follow a committed lane; Vantage alternates charges with jumpable expanding ground sweeps at low health. The HUD gives the current counterplay cue. Speed strikes and body checks retain their recovery windows.

Gamepad: A jump, B slide, X strike, Y bolt, bumpers focus/pulse, left-stick click sprint, View activity, right-stick click toggles the map, Start pause / close map. Menus support controller and keyboard navigation.

## Presentation and accessibility

- Existing authored 1.80 m crimson speedster, articulated traversal rig and wrist/heel ribbons.
- Distinct outer architecture: terraced housing, copper campus buildings, sawtooth foundries and harbour blocks; six new landmark silhouettes and a paved riverwalk.
- Revised facade palette, road markings, lighting and atmosphere; distant cells use simplified skyline geometry.
- Redesigned title, chapter select, HUD, dialogue, map, pause and result screens with visible focus states and responsive layouts.
- Internally generated footsteps, landings, wind, water, electrical abilities and city ambience. Audio begins after interaction, with volume and mute controls; pause and hidden tabs silence it.
- Reduced motion suppresses speed zoom, camera roll/shake, storm flashes, trails, replay ghosts and interface animation. Quality, sensitivity and speed-unit settings are retained.

## Validation

```bash
npm run audit
npm run typecheck
npm test
npm run build
```

CI installs the lockfile with lifecycle scripts disabled and runs all four checks. `npm run test:graphics` remains available for the original graphics regression suite.

Tests exercise geometry and textures with a CPU canvas and Babylon NullEngine; full-speed traversal, enemy counterplay, world layout/LOD, save migration, route sweeps and recordings, story branches and ambient/audio lifecycle have dedicated coverage. These tests do not establish GPU appearance, audible mix quality or a hardware frame rate. See [overhaul scope and validation](docs/OVERHAUL.md) and [graphics checklist](docs/GRAPHICS.md) before merging.

## Architecture and budgets

- Fixed player simulation at 120 Hz, with bounded catch-up; custom swept kinematic movement against a spatial hash.
- World collision stays resident. Nearby merged cells render in full; distant cells switch to cheap skyline proxies. This is rendering LOD, not asynchronous cell streaming or floating-origin rebasing.
- Ambient population uses seven thin-instanced draw batches: low/medium/high cap cars at 12/24/36 and civilians at 20/36/54. Nearby validated loops recycle as you travel; ambient entities do not become physics bodies.
- Sound uses four persistent beds, at most twelve transient voices, rate-limited cues and a master limiter.
- Replay ghosts use one merged mesh and bounded local storage; they have no collision or shadow cost.
- Babylon WebGPU selection retains a WebGL fallback. Procedural art uses independent random streams so visual edits do not reshuffle gameplay.
- Version-three profiles migrate existing progress, settings, motes and route times rather than resetting them.

The performance target remains 60 fps at 1080p on a midrange desktop, with scalable settings for slower hardware. It is a target, not a measured result for this overhaul.

## Repository policy

Keep the setting, powers, cast and presentation original. This project does not use third-party superhero characters or assets. Preserve independent free roam/story modes, test movement at top speed and under slow frames, and migrate save schemas as public data. Keep code and small source assets in Git; add Git LFS before introducing large source models, textures or audio. Record asset licences in [ASSETS.md](docs/ASSETS.md). Generated build output does not belong in Git.
